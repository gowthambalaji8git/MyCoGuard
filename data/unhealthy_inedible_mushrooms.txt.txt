UNHEALTHY / INEDIBLE OR NOT-RECOMMENDED MUSHROOMS

IMPORTANT:
"Inedible" or "not recommended" does NOT automatically mean poisonous.
Some mushroom groups contain both edible and unsafe species.
Correct species identification is essential.


1. False Morel
Uses:
- Not recommended as food

Problem:
- Some false morels contain gyromitrin.
- Gyromitrin can cause serious poisoning.

Body-related effects:
- Gastrointestinal system
- Nervous system
- Liver in severe cases

Possible symptoms:
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Abdominal pain
- Dizziness
- Seizures in severe poisoning

Details:
False morels should not be confused with true edible morels.

Source:
CDC
FDA
Poison Control


2. Ink Cap Mushroom
Uses:
- Some species have culinary uses under specific conditions.
- Unknown wild specimens should be avoided.

Problem:
- Some Coprinopsis species contain coprine.
- Coprine can produce an alcohol-related toxic reaction.

Body-related effects:
- Gastrointestinal system
- Cardiovascular effects may occur

Possible symptoms:
- Flushing
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Headache
- Palpitations
- Low blood pressure

Details:
Toxicity differs between species, so identification is important.

Source:
Merck Manual
Poison Control


3. Wood Ear Look-alikes
Uses:
- Do not consume unidentified wild specimens.

Problem:
- Misidentification may result in eating a toxic mushroom.

Body-related effects:
- Depends on the actual species.
- May affect the gastrointestinal system, nervous system, liver or kidneys.

Details:
True Auricularia species can be edible, but wild look-alikes require reliable identification.

Source:
FDA
Poison Control


4. Some Russula Species
Uses:
- Some Russula species are edible.
- Unknown species should not be eaten.

Problem:
- Certain species can cause gastrointestinal irritation.

Body-related effects:
- Mainly stomach and intestines.

Possible symptoms:
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Abdominal pain
- Diarrhea

Details:
Edibility varies by species. The genus name alone cannot determine safety.

Source:
Poison Control
Regional mycology guidance


5. Some Lactarius Species
Uses:
- Some species are edible after proper preparation.
- Some species are irritating.

Problem:
- Acrid or irritating species can cause gastrointestinal illness.

Body-related effects:
- Mainly gastrointestinal system.

Possible symptoms:
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Abdominal discomfort
- Diarrhea

Details:
Do not consume unidentified wild Lactarius specimens.

Source:
Poison Control
Regional mycology guidance


6. Some Boletus Species
Uses:
- Some boletes are edible.
- Some species are toxic.

Problem:
- Toxic species may cause gastrointestinal poisoning.
- Severity depends on the species.

Body-related effects:
- Mainly gastrointestinal system.
- Some species may produce systemic effects.

Possible symptoms:
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Abdominal pain
- Diarrhea

Details:
Never eat an unidentified bolete based only on its appearance.

Source:
FDA
Poison Control
